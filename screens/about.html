The Story Behind traces

TRACES began with the home I grew up in.

Over the years, the house has gone through changes, repairs, movements, layers, and memories, many of which were never documented in real time. Today, with discussions around future redevelopment and evacuation, there is a growing need to preserve it before parts of it are destroyed, changed, or disappear.

The project offers a digital interface for documenting homes, spaces, and objects through 3D scans, photography, image uploads, and personal writing. Users are invited to scan rooms, areas within the home, or individual objects, mark points within the scan, add memories, stories, and texts, and build a visual and personal archive of the home.

Alongside documenting the present, TRACES also allows users to upload existing materials from private archives, such as old photographs, previous scans, or documentation of things that no longer exist. This creates another layer of memory, connecting what once was, what exists now, and what is about to change.

The interface includes a timeline view, showing how spaces and objects change over time. It allows users to compare different versions of the same place and create a continuous record of the home across different periods.

TRACES also offers a shared space where other people are invited to document, preserve, and share their own homes, objects, and stories.
